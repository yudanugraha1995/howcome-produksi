Title: What If You Ate Moldy Bread? 🍞 #shorts

Description:
A green spot on your bread! 🍞😳 What if you just cut it off and ate the rest? KOMO zooms in to see what is really growing there.

🔍 HOW IT WORKS
The fuzzy spot you see is only the top of the mold. Mold is a fungus, and it grows thin threads (called hyphae) that spread down into the food like roots. In soft, moist foods like bread, those threads can travel far past the spot you can see. Some molds can also make toxins called mycotoxins. That is why food safety experts say soft moldy foods should go in the trash, even if the spot looks small.

📌 KEY FACTS
• The USDA says to throw away moldy bread and baked goods, because the mold can spread below the surface.
• Mold threads (hyphae) grow into soft food like roots, so cutting off the spot is not enough.
• Some molds produce mycotoxins, which can make people sick.
• Hard foods are different: for hard cheese or firm vegetables, you can cut away at least 1 inch (2.5 cm) around the spot.
• Do not sniff moldy food: you can breathe in spores, which can bother people with allergies.
• Mold loves warm, humid places, so bread molds faster in a warm kitchen.

✅ MOLD RULES
• Soft food (bread, soft fruit, yogurt, jam): toss it.
• Hard cheese and firm veggies: cut at least 1 inch around the spot.
• Wrap the moldy food before throwing it away, and check nearby food.
• Keep bread cool and dry; freeze slices you will not eat soon.

❓ QUICK FAQ
Q: What if I already ate a bite of moldy bread?
A: Most healthy people will be fine after a small bite. If you feel sick or have allergies or a weak immune system, talk to a doctor.
Q: Why does bread get moldy so fast?
A: Bread is soft, moist and full of food for mold, and spores are everywhere in the air.
Q: Does the fridge stop mold?
A: It slows it down; the freezer stops it.

🤯 PLOT TWIST
Some mold is amazing! Blue cheese gets its flavor from special safe molds, and penicillin, one of the first antibiotics, was discovered from mold in 1928. 🧀💊
