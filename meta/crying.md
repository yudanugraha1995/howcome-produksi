Title: What If You Cried Every Day? 😢 #shorts

Description:
Sad movie. Tears everywhere. 😢 What if you cried every single day? Is that bad, or is your body doing something smart? KOMO explains the science of tears.

🔍 HOW IT WORKS
Your eyes make tears all day long, even when you are happy. These "basal" tears keep the surface of your eyes wet, smooth and protected. "Reflex" tears flood in when something irritates your eyes, like smoke, dust or onions. "Emotional" tears come when you feel sad, happy or moved. Crying is linked to your nervous system: after a good cry, many people feel calmer, which may be your body switching from stress mode to rest mode.

📌 KEY FACTS
• There are three kinds of tears: basal (always there), reflex (irritation) and emotional.
• Tears contain water, oils, mucus, salt and proteins that help fight germs.
• Basal tears are produced all the time to keep your eyes from drying out.
• Research suggests crying can help calm you down afterwards, though not instantly.
• Crying can also be a social signal: it shows others you need support.
• Feeling sad and crying most days for weeks can be a sign of depression, and it is worth talking to someone.

✅ WHEN TEARS COME
• It is okay to cry: it is a normal human reaction.
• Give yourself a few minutes; many people feel calmer after.
• Talk to a friend, family member or professional if sadness does not go away.
• Dry, irritated eyes? Blink often and rest from screens.

❓ QUICK FAQ
Q: Why do my eyes water when I yawn?
A: Yawning squeezes the muscles around your eyes, which pushes tears out of the tear glands.
Q: Why does my nose run when I cry?
A: Extra tears drain through a small duct into your nose.
Q: Do animals cry emotional tears?
A: Many animals make tears to protect their eyes, but emotional crying seems to be mostly a human thing.

🤯 PLOT TWIST
Onions make you cry on purpose! Cutting an onion releases a gas that stings your eyes, so your eyes make reflex tears to wash it away. Chill the onion first and it stings less. 🧅
