Title: What If You Showered Before Bed Every Night? 🚿 #shorts

Description:
A warm shower right before bed... ahh. 🚿😌 What if you did it every single night? Does it really help you sleep? KOMO checks a big review of studies.

🔍 HOW IT WORKS
To fall asleep, your core body temperature needs to drop a little. A warm shower or bath brings blood to your hands and feet. When you step out, that blood flow helps heat leave your body faster, so your core temperature falls. Your brain reads that cooling as a signal that it is time to sleep.

📌 KEY FACTS
• A review of 17 studies found a warm shower or bath 1 to 2 hours before bed helped people fall asleep about 10 minutes faster.
• Water around 40 to 42.5°C (104 to 108.5°F) worked best in that review.
• Even a short shower can help, as long as it warms you up.
• Your body temperature naturally drops in the evening as part of your body clock.
• A cool bedroom helps the same cooling process.
• A very hot shower right before lights out may leave some people too warm to sleep at once.

✅ SLEEPY SHOWER ROUTINE
• Shower 1 to 2 hours before bedtime.
• Use warm, comfortable water, not scalding hot.
• Keep your bedroom cool and dark afterwards.
• Pair it with a no-screen wind-down.

❓ QUICK FAQ
Q: Morning or night shower: which is better?
A: Both are fine for hygiene; a night shower may help sleep, a morning one may help wake you up.
Q: Is going to bed with wet hair bad?
A: It will not give you a cold, but it can cause frizz and a damp pillow.
Q: Do cold showers before bed work?
A: They tend to wake people up instead.

🤯 PLOT TWIST
It is not the warmth that makes you sleepy, it is the cooling down afterwards! The drop in body temperature after you step out tells your brain it is bedtime. 🌡️
