Title: What If You Fell Asleep to Music Every Night? 🎶 #shorts

Description:
Earbuds in, playlist on, lights off. 🎶🌙 What if you fell asleep to music every night? Relaxing, or secretly ruining your sleep? KOMO checks a surprising study.

🔍 HOW IT WORKS
Music can lower stress and help some people relax at bedtime. But songs can also get stuck in your head as "earworms". Researchers found earworms can keep playing in your mind while you try to sleep, and even during the night. Your brain keeps replaying the tune, which can make sleep lighter and more broken.

📌 KEY FACTS
• In a study, people who listened to more music, especially near bedtime, had more night-time earworms.
• People with frequent night earworms slept worse in the sleep lab.
• Instrumental music caused more earworms than songs with lyrics in that study.
• Earworms are very common: most people get them every week.
• For some people, calm music still helps them relax before bed.
• Wearing earbuds all night can cause sore ears and may affect ear health.

✅ BETTER BEDTIME SOUND
• Stop the music a while before lights out.
• Try white noise, rain sounds or a podcast at low volume instead.
• Use a sleep timer so the music turns off.
• Keep the volume low and avoid earbuds all night.

❓ QUICK FAQ
Q: Is it bad to sleep with music on?
A: Not always, but music near bedtime is linked to more night earworms for some people.
Q: How do I get rid of an earworm?
A: Some studies suggest chewing gum or doing a puzzle can help.
Q: Is white noise better?
A: It has no melody to get stuck, so many people find it easier.

🤯 PLOT TWIST
Even calm instrumental music caused night earworms in the study, even more than songs with lyrics! Your brain loves to hum along. 🎵
