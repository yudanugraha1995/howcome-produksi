Title: What If Your Toothbrush Sat Next to the Toilet? 🪥 #shorts

Description:
Your toothbrush sits right next to the toilet. 😬🪥 What if it stayed there every day? KOMO looks at what lands on it after every flush.

🔍 HOW IT WORKS
When you flush with the lid up, the water swirl throws tiny droplets into the air, a "toilet plume". These droplets can carry bacteria and float around the bathroom before settling on surfaces, including toothbrushes. Bathrooms are also warm and humid, and a wet brush gives microbes a nice place to stay.

📌 KEY FACTS
• Flushing with the lid open sprays tiny droplets into the air.
• In a study of shared college bathrooms, about 60% of toothbrushes had fecal (poop) bacteria on them.
• Bacteria were found whether toothbrushes were covered or not.
• Most of these microbes are not dangerous for healthy people, but they are still not nice to brush with.
• The American Dental Association suggests rinsing your brush, storing it upright and letting it air-dry.
• Replace your toothbrush every 3 to 4 months, or sooner if the bristles are frayed.

✅ CLEAN BRUSH HABITS
• Close the toilet lid before you flush.
• Store your brush upright and let it air-dry.
• Keep it as far from the toilet as you can.
• Do not share toothbrushes.
• Replace it every 3 to 4 months.

❓ QUICK FAQ
Q: Should I keep my toothbrush in a case?
A: Closed cases keep it wet; a ventilated cover or open air-drying is better.
Q: Should I disinfect my toothbrush?
A: Rinsing and air-drying is usually enough for healthy people.
Q: Does closing the lid really help?
A: It reduces how far the droplets spread.

🤯 PLOT TWIST
A closed toothbrush case can make it worse! It keeps the brush damp, and microbes love damp places. Let it air-dry and close the lid when you flush. 🚽
