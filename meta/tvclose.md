Title: What If You Sat Close to the TV Every Day? 📺 #shorts

Description:
"Move back! You are too close to the TV!" 📺 What if you sat that close every single day? Would it really ruin your eyes? KOMO investigates the myth.

🔍 HOW IT WORKS
Kids can focus up close much more easily than adults, so many children naturally sit near the screen. Eye doctors say sitting close does not permanently damage your eyes. Staring for a long time can cause eye strain, with tired, dry or achy eyes, because people blink less while watching. That strain goes away with rest.

📌 KEY FACTS
• The American Academy of Ophthalmology says sitting close to the TV will not damage your eyes.
• Long screen time can cause temporary eye strain and dry eyes.
• A child who always sits very close might be nearsighted, so an eye check is a good idea.
• In a school study in China, about 40 extra minutes outdoors a day meant fewer kids became nearsighted.
• Nearsightedness (myopia) is rising in kids around the world.
• Daylight outdoors seems to help young eyes develop normally.

✅ HAPPY EYES WITH SCREENS
• Follow the 20-20-20 rule: every 20 minutes, look 20 feet (6 m) away for 20 seconds.
• Get kids outside every day.
• If a child always sits very close or squints, book an eye exam.
• Take screen breaks and blink.

❓ QUICK FAQ
Q: Does screen time cause myopia?
A: Lots of near work and too little time outdoors are both linked to myopia in kids.
Q: Is a phone closer than a TV worse?
A: Holding screens very close for long periods adds more near-focus work, so breaks matter.
Q: Do blue light glasses help?
A: Eye doctors say there is little evidence they prevent eye strain.

🤯 PLOT TWIST
The warning was not totally made up! In the 1960s, some color TVs gave off extra X-ray radiation and were recalled. Modern TVs are safe. 📺
