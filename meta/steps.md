Title: What If You Walked 10,000 Steps Every Day? 👟 #shorts

Description:
10,000 steps every day. 👟 Is that really the magic number? What if you walked it every single day? KOMO checks what the science says.

🔍 HOW IT WORKS
Walking gets your heart, lungs and muscles working, helps control blood sugar and is easy to fit into everyday life. Researchers combined data from about 50,000 adults wearing step counters and followed them for years. They looked at how many steps people took a day and how that related to their risk of dying early.

📌 KEY FACTS
• More daily steps were linked to a lower risk of dying early.
• For adults 60 and older, the benefit leveled off around 6,000 to 8,000 steps a day.
• For younger adults, the benefit leveled off around 8,000 to 10,000 steps a day.
• Even small increases help: going from very few steps to a few thousand more matters a lot.
• Short walks after meals can help lower blood sugar spikes.
• Health guidelines suggest about 150 minutes of moderate activity a week for adults.

✅ EASY WAYS TO ADD STEPS
• Take a 10-minute walk after meals.
• Use the stairs when you can.
• Walk while on phone calls.
• Get off the bus one stop early.
• Track your steps and add a little each week.

❓ QUICK FAQ
Q: Do I need exactly 10,000 steps?
A: No, benefits start well below that and level off around 6,000 to 10,000 depending on age.
Q: Does walking speed matter?
A: Brisk walking may add extra benefits.
Q: Are steps enough exercise?
A: Walking is great; adding some strength exercise is even better.

🤯 PLOT TWIST
The famous "10,000 steps" goal did not come from science! It came from a 1960s Japanese pedometer called "manpo-kei", which means "10,000-step meter". Every extra step still helps. 😳

📚 SOURCES

- Buffey et al., Sports Medicine (2022): light walking after meals and blood sugar
- Tudor-Locke & Bassett, Sports Medicine (2004): origin of the 10,000-step goal
