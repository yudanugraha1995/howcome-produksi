Title: What If You Chewed Ice Every Day? 🧊 #shorts

Description:
Crunch, crunch, crunch! 🧊 What if you chewed ice every single day? Your teeth might not love it as much as you do. KOMO breaks it down.

🔍 HOW IT WORKS
Tooth enamel is the hardest material in your body, but it is not built to crush hard, frozen blocks over and over. Ice is hard and very cold, and biting it puts sudden force on your teeth. Fast changes between cold and warm can also make tiny cracks grow. Over time, chewing ice can chip enamel, crack teeth, or break fillings and crowns.

📌 KEY FACTS
• The American Dental Association lists chewing ice as a habit that can crack or chip teeth.
• Ice can also damage fillings, crowns and braces.
• Tiny cracks in enamel can make teeth more sensitive to hot and cold.
• A constant craving to chew ice has a name: pagophagia.
• Pagophagia is often linked to iron-deficiency anemia, and the craving can fade once iron levels are treated.
• One idea is that chewing ice may give a little alertness boost to people who are low on iron.

✅ IF YOU LOVE THE CRUNCH
• Let ice melt in your mouth instead of biting it.
• Try crushed or shaved ice, which is softer on teeth.
• Swap in a crunchy snack like carrots or apples.
• If the craving is strong and constant, ask a doctor to check your iron.

❓ QUICK FAQ
Q: Is crushed ice safer than cubes?
A: It is softer on your teeth, but letting ice melt is the safest.
Q: Why do I crave ice?
A: Sometimes it is just a habit or for cooling down, but a constant craving can be linked to iron deficiency.
Q: Can chewing ice break braces?
A: Yes, it can damage brackets and wires.

🤯 PLOT TWIST
A strong urge to chew ice can be a clue from your body: it is often linked to low iron! If you cannot stop crunching, a simple blood test can check. 🩸
