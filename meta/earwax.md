Title: What If You Never Cleaned Your Ears? 👂 #shorts

Description:
Ear wax. Kind of gross, right? 👂 What if you never cleaned your ears at all? KOMO reveals why your ears might be smarter than your cotton swab.

🔍 HOW IT WORKS
Ear wax (cerumen) is made by glands in your ear canal. It traps dust and dirt, keeps the skin moist and has substances that help fight germs. The skin in your ear canal slowly grows outward like a conveyor belt, and movements from chewing and talking help push old wax out. For most people, the ears clean themselves.

📌 KEY FACTS
• Ear wax protects your ears by trapping dust and helping fight germs.
• Ear canals clean themselves for most people.
• Cotton swabs can push wax deeper and pack it against the eardrum.
• Doctors warn never to put small objects deep into your ear canal.
• Some people make more wax and may need a doctor to remove a blockage.
• Wet or dry ear wax type is mostly decided by a single gene, ABCC11.

✅ EAR CARE THE RIGHT WAY
• Clean only the outer ear with a washcloth.
• Skip cotton swabs inside the ear canal.
• Blocked, muffled, or painful ear? See a doctor instead of digging.
• Avoid "ear candles": they do not work and can burn you.

❓ QUICK FAQ
Q: Is ear wax dirty?
A: No, it is a natural protective layer, not a sign of poor hygiene.
Q: Why does my ear feel blocked?
A: It can be a wax buildup, water, or an infection; a doctor can check safely.
Q: Do I need to clean my ears every day?
A: No, just the outer ear when you wash.

🤯 PLOT TWIST
Your ear wax type is in your genes! One small change in a single gene decides wet or dry wax, and most people in East Asia have the dry, flaky kind. 🧬
