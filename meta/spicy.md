Title: What If You Ate Spicy Food Every Day? 🌶️ #shorts

Description:
Your mouth is on fire, your nose is running, you are sweating... 🌶️🔥 What if you ate spicy food every single day? Hurting you or helping you? KOMO checks a study of half a million people.

🔍 HOW IT WORKS
The heat in chili peppers comes from capsaicin. It does not actually burn you: it switches on a heat-sensing receptor in your mouth called TRPV1, the same one that reacts to real heat. Your brain thinks your mouth is on fire, so you sweat, your nose runs and your face turns red. With regular spicy food, people often get used to it, so the same chili feels less hot over time.

📌 KEY FACTS
• A study of about 500,000 adults in China found people who ate spicy food almost every day had about 14% lower risk of death during the study period.
• That is a link, not proof: spicy eaters may differ in other ways.
• Capsaicin triggers the TRPV1 heat receptor, which is why chili feels hot.
• Spicy food can trigger heartburn or stomach discomfort in some people.
• Your tolerance can grow when you eat spicy food often.
• The 2021 Nobel Prize in Medicine was awarded partly for discovering how TRPV1 senses heat, using capsaicin.

✅ HANDLING THE HEAT
• Milk or yogurt cools the burn better than water.
• Rice or bread can help soak up the heat.
• Build up slowly if you want to handle more spice.
• Heartburn or stomach pain after spicy food? Ease off and talk to a doctor if it keeps happening.

❓ QUICK FAQ
Q: Can spicy food cause stomach ulcers?
A: No, most ulcers are caused by bacteria (H. pylori) or certain medicines, not chili.
Q: Why does water not help?
A: Capsaicin is oily, so water just spreads it around.
Q: Why do birds eat chilies without pain?
A: Birds do not feel capsaicin the way mammals do.

🤯 PLOT TWIST
Water will not save you! Capsaicin is oily, so water spreads it around. Milk contains a protein called casein that helps wash it away. 🥛
