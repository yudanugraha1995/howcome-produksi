Title: What If You Ate Super Fast Every Day? 🍜 #shorts

Description:
Plate empty in 3 minutes flat! 🍜💨 What if you ate super fast every single day? KOMO explains why your stomach and brain might not keep up.

🔍 HOW IT WORKS
When you eat, your stomach and gut release hormones that tell your brain "I am getting full". That message takes time, often around 20 minutes, to fully arrive. If you eat very fast, you can finish a big meal before the "full" signal reaches your brain, so you keep eating more than you need. Fast eating also means less chewing and more swallowed air.

📌 KEY FACTS
• Fullness signals from your gut to your brain take time to build up.
• A large review found fast eaters were more likely to gain weight than slow eaters.
• Eating slowly is linked to eating fewer calories at a meal in several studies.
• Chewing more helps break down food and gives your body more time to feel full.
• Eating very fast can cause bloating, burping and heartburn for some people.
• Distractions like phones and TV can make you eat faster without noticing.

✅ SLOW DOWN TRICKS
• Put your fork or spoon down between bites.
• Chew each bite a little longer.
• Take sips of water during the meal.
• Eat without scrolling for at least part of the meal.

❓ QUICK FAQ
Q: How long should a meal take?
A: Aiming for about 20 minutes gives fullness signals time to arrive.
Q: Is eating fast bad for digestion?
A: It can cause bloating or heartburn in some people because you swallow more air.
Q: Does chewing more really help?
A: Studies suggest more chewing can reduce how much people eat.

🤯 PLOT TWIST
The easiest fix is tiny: put your fork down between bites! Small pauses give your stomach time to send the "full" message. 🍴
