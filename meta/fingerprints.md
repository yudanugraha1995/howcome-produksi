Title: What If You Had No Fingerprints? 🫆 #shorts

Description:
Look at your fingertips. 🔍 Loops, whorls, arches... What if those swirls just disappeared? KOMO looks at the real people born without fingerprints.

🔍 HOW IT WORKS
Fingerprints form before you are born, while your skin is developing in the womb. The ridges are shaped by genes and by tiny differences in pressure and growth, which is why even identical twins have different fingerprints. The ridges may help you grip things and feel fine textures, because they change how your skin vibrates when you touch a surface.

📌 KEY FACTS
• Fingerprints form before birth and stay the same pattern for life.
• Even identical twins have different fingerprints.
• A very rare condition called adermatoglyphia means being born with no fingerprints.
• Scientists linked adermatoglyphia to a change in a gene called SMARCAD1, found in one family.
• People in that family were sometimes nicknamed as having "immigration delay disease" because fingerprint checks failed at borders.
• Fingerprint ridges may help us sense fine textures through tiny vibrations.

✅ CHECK YOUR OWN PRINTS
• Look at your fingertip with your phone camera zoomed in.
• Loops are the most common pattern, then whorls; arches are rarer.
• Press a fingertip on a glass to see your print.
• Compare fingers: each one can be different!

❓ QUICK FAQ
Q: Can fingerprints change?
A: The pattern stays the same, but cuts, burns or lots of hand work can wear ridges down.
Q: Do twins share fingerprints?
A: No, even identical twins have different prints.
Q: Why do we have fingerprints?
A: Scientists think they help with touch and possibly grip.

🤯 PLOT TWIST
Koalas have fingerprints too, and they look so much like ours that experts said they could be confused at a crime scene! 🐨
