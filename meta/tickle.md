Title: What If You Tried to Tickle Yourself? 🤭 #shorts

Description:
Tickle, tickle! 🤭 Why can someone else make you laugh like crazy, but you cannot tickle yourself? KOMO explains the brain trick behind it.

🔍 HOW IT WORKS
Your brain is a prediction machine. When you move your own hand, a part of your brain called the cerebellum predicts exactly what that touch will feel like. Because the sensation is expected, your brain turns it down. A tickle needs surprise, so your own fingers just do not work. When someone else tickles you, the touch is unpredictable, and your brain reacts fully.

📌 KEY FACTS
• The cerebellum predicts the feeling of your own movements.
• Self-made touch feels weaker than the same touch from someone else.
• Brain scans show less activity in touch areas when you touch yourself.
• Tickling makes people laugh, but many do not actually enjoy it.
• Rats laugh too when tickled, with high-pitched chirps humans cannot hear.
• Some people with certain conditions, like schizophrenia, can sometimes tickle themselves.

✅ TRY IT YOURSELF
• Tickle your palm, then ask a friend to do the same: notice the difference.
• Try with your eyes closed.
• Ask before tickling someone: not everyone likes it!

❓ QUICK FAQ
Q: Why do we laugh when tickled?
A: Scientists think it may be a social reflex that helps bonding, especially in kids.
Q: Where are people most ticklish?
A: Common spots are the ribs, armpits, neck and feet.
Q: Can you stop being ticklish?
A: Not completely, but staying calm and expecting it can make it feel weaker.

🤯 PLOT TWIST
In one experiment, a robot copied people moves with a tiny delay, and suddenly self-tickling worked! Add surprise, and your brain cannot predict it anymore. 🤖
