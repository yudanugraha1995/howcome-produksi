Title: What If You Napped Every Afternoon? 😴 #shorts

Description:
2 PM and your eyes keep closing... 😴 What if you napped every afternoon? Lazy or genius? KOMO checks what NASA found.

🔍 HOW IT WORKS
Your body clock naturally makes most people a bit sleepy in the early afternoon, the so-called post-lunch dip. A short nap lets your brain clear some of the built-up sleep pressure. The trick is length: short naps (about 10 to 20 minutes) keep you in light sleep, so you wake up refreshed. Longer naps can drop you into deep sleep, and waking from that leaves you groggy, a feeling called sleep inertia.

📌 KEY FACTS
• NASA studied long-haul airline pilots: a planned ~26-minute nap boosted performance by 34% and alertness by 54%.
• The early-afternoon dip is part of your natural daily rhythm, not just lunch.
• Naps of about 10 to 20 minutes are usually best for a quick boost.
• Naps longer than about 30 minutes can cause grogginess (sleep inertia).
• Napping late in the day can make it harder to fall asleep at night.
• Feeling very sleepy every day despite good night sleep is worth checking with a doctor.

✅ THE PERFECT NAP
• Keep it short: set an alarm for 20 minutes.
• Nap in the early afternoon, not in the evening.
• Make it dark and quiet, or use an eye mask.
• Try a coffee nap if you are an adult who drinks coffee.

❓ QUICK FAQ
Q: Is napping a sign of laziness?
A: No, short naps are a well-studied way to boost alertness.
Q: Why do I wake up groggy?
A: You probably slept too long and woke from deep sleep.
Q: Can naps replace night sleep?
A: No, they help, but they cannot fully replace a good night of sleep.

🤯 PLOT TWIST
The coffee nap! Drink a coffee, then nap about 15 minutes right away. Caffeine needs time to kick in, so you wake up just as it starts working. Studies with sleepy drivers found it worked better than coffee or a nap alone. ☕
