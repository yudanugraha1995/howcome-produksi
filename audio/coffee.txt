One cup in the morning, one after lunch, maybe one more at four. What if you drank coffee every single day? Is it slowly hurting you, or quietly helping you?

Coffee is one of the most popular drinks on Earth. Billions of cups are poured every single day, from tiny espressos to giant iced lattes.

Caffeine blocks a sleepy signal in your brain called adenosine. But it stays around for hours. Half of it can still be in your body five hours later.

Now the big news. A huge review of more than two hundred studies found that people who drank about three to four cups a day had a lower risk of dying early and of heart disease, compared to people who drank none. That's a link, not proof, but it's a strong clue. Just don't load it with tons of sugar and cream.

Plot twist! Coffee doesn't really dry you out. In one study, men drank either coffee or water for days, and their hydration was about the same. So your morning cup still counts as a drink. Just stop by mid afternoon if you want to sleep well tonight.
