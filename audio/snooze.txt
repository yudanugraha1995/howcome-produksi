Beep beep beep. Snooze. Beep beep beep. Snooze again. What if you hit snooze every single morning? Is it ruining your brain, or is it totally fine?

You're not alone. In one survey, about seven in ten people said they snooze at least sometimes, often for twenty minutes or more.

Snoozing gets a bad name because your sleep keeps breaking. And that groggy feeling is real. It's called sleep inertia.

But scientists tested it. People snoozed for thirty minutes, then took brain tests. Their sleep barely changed, and their thinking was just as good, sometimes even a bit sharper, than people who got up right away. Their stress hormone in the morning looked normal too. So those extra minutes weren't wrecking anything.

Plot twist! Snoozing isn't the villain. Losing sleep is. If you snooze because you went to bed way too late, the real fix is an earlier bedtime. Your alarm is just the messenger. And if you love snoozing, set one alarm a bit later instead of five.
