Ouch! Your mouth is on fire, your nose is running, and you're sweating. What if you ate spicy food every single day? Is it hurting you, or helping you?

Chili heat comes from a chemical called capsaicin. Birds can't feel it at all, so they happily eat peppers and spread the seeds.

Capsaicin tricks a heat sensor in your mouth. Your brain thinks you're really burning, so you sweat and turn red. But nothing is burned.

Now the surprise. A study of about half a million adults in China found that people who ate spicy food almost every day had about a fourteen percent lower risk of dying during the study. That doesn't prove chili makes you live longer, but it's a fun clue. Chili lovers, this one is for you.

Plot twist! Water won't save you at all. Capsaicin is oily, so water just spreads it around. Milk works better, because its protein helps wash it away. And that rush you feel after the pain? Your brain may release feel good chemicals to fight the burn. No wonder some people get hooked!
